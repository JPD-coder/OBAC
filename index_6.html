HVAC pricing shouldn't be a secret.

Replacing an air-conditioning system is one of the largest expenses a homeowner can face. Yet most consumers have almost no idea what the equipment actually costs, what a reasonable installation should cost, or how much profit is built into the price they're quoted.

We think you should know.

We spent years inside the HVAC industry. We understand how equipment is purchased, how installations are priced, how contractors calculate gross margins — and how two homeowners can receive very different prices for essentially the same equipment and installation.

We're making that knowledge public.

      How do we know?
      
We were in the business.

      
Here are the dirty little secrets — and the real reasons prices vary.

    


    

      
Why you can't tell a good price from a bad one

      
    
        
  • The cost is no mystery — to them.Equipment, labor, materials, permit, overhead. The contractor knows every number in the job.
    • 
        
  • You get one number."Your new system will cost $____." Nothing behind it, and about an hour to decide.
    • 
        
  • There's no MSRP.A car has a sticker you can look up. HVAC has no published list, no invoice price, no reference point anywhere.
    • 
        
  • You don't have an uncle in the business.Nobody to call for a sanity check before signing a five-figure contract.
    • 
        
  • Your neighbor can't help either.They bought in 2015, different size, different refrigerant era, possibly on a payment plan they never converted to a total.
    • 
        
  • You buy one every ten years.Four in a lifetime. Never enough repetition to learn what anything costs.
    • 
      

    


    

      
Why two people pay different prices

      
    
        
  • Price doesn't always follow cost.It follows the market, the neighborhood, the home value, the zip code, and what they believe you'll tolerate.
    • 
        
  • "Over the bridge."The trade's own shorthand for the side of town where customers are expected to pay more. The air conditioner didn't cost more because it crossed a bridge — the customer was simply charged more.
    • 
        
  • The salesperson is paid to close, not to fit.Commission runs 10–20% at a large dealer — the most negotiable line in your quote, and nobody mentions it's there. It's worst with the companies you find on the internet: a booked lead can cost them hundreds of dollars, so they have to close and absorb both the marketing and the commission. All of it is passed to you — along with the debt service, if they're private-equity owned.
    • 
      

    


    

      
What a fair profit actually looks like

      

        We're not against contractors making money — quite the opposite. They carry real
        costs you never see: employees, trucks, insurance, licensing, training, warranty
        obligations, office staff, inventory. You want them healthy enough in five years to
        answer the phone when your system faults. But margins vary dramatically. Two
        contractors installing comparable equipment can land on very different prices, and
        two customers of the same contractor may not get the same economics. There's a
        difference between a reasonable profit and a price nobody can justify — helping you
        see that difference is the whole point of this site.
